\title{Pilot-Limited Phase Control for a Contiguous Tiled Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface: A Controlled Simulation Study}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
For a single-antenna transmitter and receiver with supplied cascaded channel estimates, RIS phase alignment has a closed-form solution that each tile can evaluate locally. Federation is therefore unnecessary for that deterministic optimization problem. We instead study inference from a limited number of receiver pilot responses on one contiguous 1024-element surface. Passive reflecting elements do not measure channel coefficients: the receiver measures the configured probes and feeds back their complex responses. The comparisons include local noisy-estimate alignment, best-probe selection, linear estimation followed by alignment, local models without federation, one- and five-round federation, and centralized learners under two explicitly matched update budgets. Training-label acquisition and model-transfer payloads are accounted for separately. Full-aperture channel generation, independent validation, and seed-level confidence intervals replace the previous distributed-panel simulation. Gains over a blocked direct link quantify the benefit of the surface, not a learning contribution. This study tests whether federation improves the measured performance--overhead tradeoff; it does not presume an advantage over local or centralized controls.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Reconfigurable intelligent surface, pilot feedback, channel acquisition, federated learning, local phase alignment.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
A programmable reflecting surface can improve a blocked wireless link by coherently combining its reflected paths. That physical array gain must be distinguished from the contribution of a phase-control algorithm. In particular, the known-channel single-input single-output (SISO) problem does not require distributed optimization: every element can independently align its cascaded coefficient with a common direct-path phase reference.

The research question here is narrower: with only $M$ receiver probe responses for $N$ reflecting elements, can a data-derived prior improve phase control at the same acquisition budget, and does federating that prior improve upon independent local learning? The first question concerns incomplete observations. The second requires a separate comparison with a local model and a local statistical estimator. Solving the first does not establish a reason to federate.

Passive channel acquisition is a substantive part of RIS design~\cite{zheng2022survey}. Sensor-assisted surfaces are another possibility: sparse active sensing elements can supply measurements to a controller~\cite{taha2019}. The present pilot experiment uses receiver feedback and a fully passive reflecting aperture. We do not attribute receive chains or per-element channel measurements to passive tiles.

The contributions of this revision are a reproducible, contiguous-aperture benchmark; an explicit distinction between supplied-CSI and limited-pilot experiments; controlled learning comparisons with acquisition and communication accounting; and falsifiable criteria for a learning advantage. Numerical superiority, privacy protection, implemented circuit efficiency, and a measured communication crossover are not assumed contributions.

\section{System and Observation Models}
\subsection{One contiguous physical surface}
A $32\times32$ uniform planar array is partitioned into a $4\times4$ grid of contiguous $8\times8$ tiles. At 28\,GHz, the pitch is $\lambda/2=5.36$\,mm. The cell-edge aperture is approximately $0.1714\times0.1714$\,m; the outermost element centres span $31\lambda/2$ on each axis. The surface centre is $(5,0,1.5)$\,m and the transmitter is at $(5,10,1.5)$\,m. Receiver positions are uniform in $[0.5,9.5]\times[1,9.5]\times[1,2]$\,m.

For each scene, the channels are generated over the whole aperture and then sliced into tiles. Line-of-sight (LoS) phases use exact transmitter--element and element--receiver distances. Each hop has five non-LoS plane waves with directions and random gains shared across the aperture. The Rician factor is 10\,dB. Close-in path-loss exponents are 2.5 on the reflecting hops and 3.5 on the direct path, with 30\,dB additional direct-path blockage. Each element has the aperture-factor assumption $G_e=4\pi(\lambda/2)^2/\lambda^2=\pi$ per hop. This is a synthetic propagation model, not a calibrated electromagnetic or ray-tracing model. Mutual coupling and receiver mobility within a coherence block are omitted.

Tiles separated by centimetres have similar large-scale propagation statistics in this geometry. We do not impose metre-scale tile displacement, independent tile scatterers, or artificial spatial data skew. The physical panel size does not establish a silicon die size or an on-chip interconnect energy model.

For tile $t$ and element $n$, let $c_{t,n}$ denote the cascaded channel. With unit-modulus reflections,
\begin{equation}
 h_{\rm eff}=h_d+\sum_{t,n}c_{t,n}e^{j\theta_{t,n}},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{P_t}{\sigma^2}|h_{\rm eff}|^2.
\end{equation}
The noise power is $-90$\,dBm. The principal operating point uses $P_t=30$\,dBm, so $10\log_{10}(P_t/\sigma^2)=120$\,dB. The latter is a transmit-to-noise ratio before propagation, not received SNR.

\subsection{Supplied-CSI diagnostic and local optimum}
The triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 |h_{\rm eff}|\leq |h_d|+\sum_{t,n}|c_{t,n}|.
\end{equation}
Equality holds for $\theta_{t,n}=\angle h_d-\angle c_{t,n}$. If $h_d=0$, any common reference phase suffices. Consequently, each tile needs only its own coefficients and one shared reference scalar. No CSI pooling, training, model exchange, or iterative optimization is required. With noisy estimates, the deployable plug-in rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 \hat\theta_{t,n}=\angle\hat h_d-\angle\hat c_{t,n}.
\end{equation}
This maximizes the estimated-channel objective; it is not necessarily the Bayes-optimal rule under uncertainty. With perfect estimates it equals the oracle exactly.

The diagnostic supplies $\hat c=c+e$ with complex Gaussian error power $\epsilon\mathbb{E}|c|^2$. The same direct estimate is supplied to every tile. All compared controllers receive the same estimates; noise realizations are coupled across $\epsilon$ by scaling the same standard noise. At $\epsilon=1$, signal and error powers are equal: the estimate is not pure noise. A passive tile cannot acquire these coefficients by itself. This diagnostic abstracts acquisition; a physical implementation would require feedback-derived estimates or an explicitly sensor-equipped architecture, with associated costs.

\subsection{Limited-pilot passive acquisition}
The passive aperture applies known phase patterns $\phi_{m,n}$ while the transmitter sends pilots. The receiver, which has a receive chain, measures and feeds back
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pilots}
 y_m=h_d+\sum_n c_ne^{j\phi_{m,n}}+w_m,
 \quad w_m\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1/\rho),\quad \rho=P_t/\sigma^2.
\end{equation}
The observations are normalized by the transmitted pilot amplitude. Define $x=[h_d,c_1,\ldots,c_N]^T$ and $A_{m,:}=[1,e^{j\phi_{m,1}},\ldots,e^{j\phi_{m,N}}]$. Then $y=Ax+w$. For $M<N+1$, $A$ has a nontrivial nullspace; in general the full channel and its elementwise phase rule cannot be recovered uniquely from $y$. This observation constraint, rather than a nonseparable SISO objective, is the potential role for a prior.

All equal-pilot-budget methods receive exactly the same $M$ noisy complex responses and fixed codebook. Learned inference also receives normalized tile coordinates. It receives no receiver position, per-element test CSI, or true direct-path phase. Inputs rotate the responses by $-\angle y_1$, normalize their RMS, and include estimated probe power relative to noise. This removes a common channel rotation, to which the optimal reflection phases are invariant. Predicted phases are absolute; no oracle phase is added after inference.

\section{Controls and Learning Protocol}
\subsection{Baselines that determine whether learning helps}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Local estimated-CSI MRC}: Eq.~\eqref{eq:local} in the supplied-CSI diagnostic, with zero model-training communication.
\item \emph{Best observed probe}: select the measured configuration with maximum $|y_m|$.
\item \emph{LMMSE followed by MRC}: estimate the channel covariance and mean from the same measured training labels, then use $\hat x=\mu+RA^H(ARA^H+\rho^{-1}I)^{-1}(y-A\mu)$. Row-stored samples use $R=X_c^TX_c^*/(S-1)$, with diagonal loading.
\item \emph{Local linear estimator followed by MRC}: each tile independently regresses its own channel labels, including the direct coefficient, on the shared receiver feedback. No labels from other tiles or federated rounds are required. Ridge loading is $10^{-3}$ of mean input covariance power. This is empirical linear regression, not an oracle-covariance bound.
\item \emph{Full-probe LS followed by local MRC}: $N+1$ orthogonal DFT probes yield channel-estimation variance $1/[\rho(N+1)]$ per coefficient. This is a higher-acquisition-cost control, explicitly distinguished from equal-$M$ methods.
\item \emph{Local neural models}, one- and five-round FedAvg, longer FedAvg, and two centralized training budgets.
\item \emph{Perfect-CSI MRC}: an unattainable-information reference in the pilot experiment; its zero charged acquisition cost must not be read as a deployable performance point.
\end{itemize}
The projected-gradient and linear-surrogate routines are numerical controls for the same SISO objective. The former applies normalized phase-gradient ascent; the latter aligns each coefficient with the current effective channel and damps the update before unit-circle projection. Neither implements alternating active/passive MISO beamforming or multiuser weighted-sum-rate optimization. Their agreement in SISO is expected. They are not counted as two distinct state-of-the-art formulations. SDR and ADMM are not evaluated in this revision and support no performance claim.

\subsection{Training information is acquired, not free}
Training and validation scenes each use $M$ feedback probes and a separate $N+1$-probe DFT sequence to obtain noisy channel labels. Labels do not use simulator truth. True channels are reserved for scoring held-out scenes and the explicitly labelled oracle. For $S$ training and $V$ validation scenes, the training-data acquisition cost is $(S+V)(M+N+1)$ pilot symbols. This is a substantial offline acquisition assumption, not a hardware-free self-supervised training claim.

The pilot learner is a two-hidden-layer MLP with width 128 and a sine/cosine output pair per element. This architecture is explicitly different from the earlier 315,906-parameter GAT. No previous GAT ranking or GAT numerical result is transferred to this experiment. Each tile minimizes its own genie-normalized rate loss computed from its measured training-channel labels. This local surrogate is not the gradient of whole-surface rate; that mismatch is a limitation to measure, not a coupled optimization claim.

FedAvg and the centralized/local models start from identical weights. They use Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 64, and constant learning rates. The pilot runs use one local epoch-equivalent per round. An epoch-equivalent comprises $\lceil S/64\rceil$ uniformly sampled minibatches; it is not guaranteed to visit every example exactly once. FedAvg resets Adam state on each broadcast. Centralized and independent local training retain optimizer state. These differences are disclosed, so the comparison is not described as differing only in data location.

If federation spends $R$ rounds with $K$ updates per tile per round and $T$ tiles, centralized controls receive both $RK$ updates (one client's serial budget) and $TRK$ updates (total network computation). The latter is the primary total-update control. Local models each receive $RK$ updates. Validation selects checkpoints without consulting test results.

The initial budget is 100 rounds, extended to a cap of 300 if no validation plateau is reached, with at least 20 rounds and a stopping rule of no validation improvement of $10^{-4}$ for 15 rounds. The saved record reports the full validation trajectory, selected round, actual update counts, and stopping reason. A validation plateau establishes a stopping condition only; it is not a proof of optimization convergence. Reaching the maximum without a plateau is reported as budget exhaustion.

\section{Evaluation and Accounting}
Each full wireless run uses 600 training scenes, 200 independent validation scenes, and 600 test scenes, with seeds 42, 123, 456, 789, and 1024. Supplied-CSI power sweeps use $P_t=-10$ to $30$\,dBm; all power axes display dBm. Targets outside this window are reported as unreached, with no extrapolated transmit-power-saving claim. Every controller is scored on the same true held-out channels for a seed. Reported confidence intervals use the seed as the independent unit. Paired differences use matched seeds. The fixed pilot codebook seed is 2024; intervals do not quantify variation over codebook design or alternative propagation models.

We report mean received SNR, $\mathbb{E}[\log_2(1+\gamma)]$, conditional-AWGN QPSK BER averaged over fading, and outage. These PHY metrics assume coherent receiver detection; common receiver synchronization and data-channel estimation overhead are not included. The pilot-adjusted rate is
\begin{equation}
 R_{\rm net}=\max\{0,1-M_{\rm used}/L\}\,
             \mathbb{E}[\log_2(1+\gamma)],\quad L=2048.
\end{equation}
Here $M_{\rm used}=N+1$ for full-probe LS and $M$ for equal-budget methods. Feedback latency, feedback errors, training amortization, and controller computation are not included in this scalar rate; their payloads or limitations are stated separately.

Robustness is assessed by absolute performance, fraction of oracle power, and paired gaps to local baselines. A smaller decrease from a worse starting point is not evidence of denoising. No prior-induced robustness mechanism is asserted without an equal-observation comparison that demonstrates it.

For array scaling, the reflected-only power is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\rm refl}(N)=\left|\sum_{n=1}^N c_ne^{j\theta_n}\right|^2.
\end{equation}
The $N^2$ reference is anchored to reflected-only oracle power at 64 elements. Direct-plus-reflected power is reported separately. A uniform-amplitude reference including the direct path is $(|h_d|+N\bar a)^2$, evaluated per scene. This prevents direct-path dominance at small $N$ from producing a false departure from the coherent-combining law.

Numerical model exchange is float32, so parameter-server endpoint payloads are $2TRP\cdot4$ bytes for $P$ parameters. In the supplied-CSI diagnostic, a hypothetical one-time pooled upload of local complex CSI is $TS(N_t+1)\cdot8$ bytes. In the passive experiment, the controller already receives the acquisition feedback and reconstructs the training labels, so centralized training requires no additional upload. Distributed training requires delivery of the limited-probe feedback and each tile's label slice, including the direct coefficient: $(S+V)T(M+N_t+1)\cdot8$ endpoint bytes before model exchange. The pooled dataset size is reported only as a reference. Local MRC and independent local training have zero federated model-transfer payload. A formula equating model exchange and the supplied-CSI upload gives an analytical crossover only; it does not establish a passive-system crossover. We do not claim a measured operating advantage at a crossover that was not run. Receiver feedback to the controller and its delivery to tiles are separate communication costs. All tiles belong to one owner; data locality is not a privacy guarantee or an adversarial threat model.

\subsection{Analytical interconnect sensitivity}
We evaluate an assumed colocated digital-controller network independently of the RF aperture. The mapping of controllers to silicon, inter-chip links, and phase-control wires to the physical panel is not implemented. The study therefore supplies conditional traffic estimates, not a claim that the 17\,cm aperture occupies one die.

Each link and endpoint port transfers 128 bits per cycle at assumed clocks of 1 and 2\,GHz, giving 128 and 256\,Gb/s. A 128-bit flit is also a documented configurable default in Garnet~\cite{garnet}; this does not validate our clock, pipeline, or circuit assumptions. We assume two router cycles and one link cycle per logical hop. The estimator is analytical, not discrete-event or cycle-accurate. For phase $q$, with directed-link loads $L_{e,q}$ and endpoint injection/ejection loads $I_{v,q},E_{v,q}$ in bits, it computes
\begin{equation}
 \hat t_q=\max\!\left\{\max_e\frac{L_{e,q}}{B},
 \max_v\frac{I_{v,q}}{B_{\rm ep}},\max_v\frac{E_{v,q}}{B_{\rm ep}}\right\}
 +H_q\frac{c_{\rm link}+c_{\rm router}}{f}.
\end{equation}
Transfers round up to whole flits. Sequential phases add. This model omits queue evolution, virtual channels, arbitration, credits, headers, reductions, and physical wire delay; the result is an estimate, not a validated bound or cycle trace. No Noxim cross-validation has been performed. Technology-node, area, circuit-power, and energy values are unreported because no implemented or calibrated circuit supports them.

The topologies are mesh, torus, folded torus, binary tree, ring, hypercube, and an explicit directed radix-2 butterfly. The previously named butterfly was an XOR hypercube; the corrected butterfly has 80 auxiliary stage vertices for 16 terminal clients. It is not an equal-area alternative to the direct networks. Torus and folded torus have identical predictions without a physical wire-layout model. Protocols include ParameterServer, full-message recursive doubling, RingAllReduce, and fixed-budget gossip. Gossip does not establish exact global averaging and is excluded from equivalent-collective claims. RingAllReduce uses a stated numeric-rank ring and retains remainder bytes; no optimal embedding is asserted.

For this sensitivity study the server is colocated at tile 0, so ParameterServer exchanges $2(T-1)RP\cdot4$ payload bytes; the earlier endpoint-accounting reference $2TRP\cdot4$ uses a separate controller. At 128\,Gb/s, a 150,528-byte model and 20 rounds require 5.644800\,ms of server-endpoint serialization in every topology, plus 0.000480--0.000960\,ms of traversal. Thus a diameter reduction does not produce a proportional latency reduction. Butterfly plus RingAllReduce is evaluated explicitly in Table~\ref{tab:noc}. The saved sweep also replays every retained pilot run's measured model size and actual round count. Sub-millisecond estimates for selected model/protocol budgets are not measurements of training time or proof of a useful federation operating point.

\section{Limitations and Scope}
The limited-pilot observation model makes estimation nontrivial but does not by itself make federation useful. Every tile receives the same pilot feedback, tile statistics are similar on a contiguous surface, and an unfederated tile can fit a local estimator. The synthetic training distribution, finite label SNR, codebook, and local training surrogate can all limit performance. A claim of generalization beyond them requires further experiments.

The earlier circular-panel results, deployment-saturation interpretation, robustness superiority claim, and unqualified ``price of locality'' interpretation are withdrawn. The former mixed-scale architecture and interconnect tables are superseded by the explicitly scoped diagnostics above. No GAT-versus-Transformer or FedAvg/FedProx/SCAFFOLD ranking from those legacy runs is retained. This is a wireless-systems simulation manuscript, not an ISCAS circuit contribution. The generic IEEE journal layout does not establish compliance with a selected venue's length or novelty requirements; no venue acceptance or submission is implied.

\section{Conclusion}
Known-channel SISO phase alignment is already local and closed form. A defensible learning experiment must instead state how the controller acquires its information and compare against local estimators at the same pilot budget. The revised simulator and controls provide that test on one contiguous aperture, with measured training budgets and explicit costs. The resulting evidence, including negative results, determines whether a federated method has a useful operating point.
